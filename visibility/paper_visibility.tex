\begin{abstract}
Projecting a transformer hidden state, or a difference of hidden
states, through the unembedding matrix $\WU$ and reading the top-$k$
tokens is a standard inspection technique. We ask how much of the
state's energy must be aligned with a token direction for that token
to appear in the top $k$. Under an isotropy assumption on the
remainder and on the rows of $\WU$, the top-$k$ readout is a
max-statistic and the answer is a closed form:
$\fstar = 2\ln(2V)/(d + 2\ln(2V))$, about $0.9\%$ of the state's
energy for Phi-2 ($d{=}2560$, $V{=}51{,}200$). We test this with
planted signals on ten models from five families (Pythia, Phi,
GPT-2, BLOOM, Qwen, TinyLlama; $d = 512$--$2560$,
$V = 32$k--$251$k). On the eight models whose unembedding passes the
isotropy gate, the measured half-visibility point $f_{50}$ tracks
$\fstar$ with slope $1.15$ in the log--log regression
($R^2 = 0.98$), with a family-dependent constant
$f_{50}/\fstar \in [0.56, 0.70]$ ($0.56 \pm 0.003$ within the
Pythia/Phi subset); the ceiling's predicted rise with $\ln V$ is
directionally confirmed across the $8\times$ vocabulary span. The
two smallest Pythia models violate the assumption: their unembedding
row directions are heavy-tailed (bath excess kurtosis $+1.1$ and
$+2.2$ versus $\approx 0$), the empirical ceiling is about twice the
isotropic prediction, and the threshold rises accordingly (for
Pythia-70m, a planted signal carrying $30\%$ of the state's energy is
usually invisible). Averaging $N$ independent difference vectors that
share a signal lowers the threshold roughly $N$-fold, as predicted.
Real hidden states violate the isotropy of the remainder as well
(post-signal kurtosis $2.8$--$19.5$), raising the effective bar for
real reads by $1.05$--$1.5\times$ on the gate-passing models. This is a
measured correction, not a failure of the framework. A battery of
eight real contrast pairs clears the bar at $4.5$--$16\times$ on
every isotropic model ($24/24$ present, surviving the real-remainder
correction) and is absent on both anisotropic models ($16/16$). The practical readings are: a
clean top-$k$ readout is compatible with the large majority of the
state's energy being unaligned with any token direction; absence
from the top $k$ bounds aligned energy only up to $\fstar$; and the
threshold is a property of $\WU$ geometry, not of $d$ alone, so
small-model readouts require a measured calibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020} and its descendants project
hidden states through the unembedding matrix $\WU \in
\mathbb{R}^{V\times d}$ (we write $\hat{\WU}$ for its row-normalised
version) and read the top-$k$ tokens. The same readout is applied to
weight vectors such as MLP output columns \citep{geva2022,dar2022}.
Differences of hidden states are not new either: contrastive and
steering methods \citep{zou2023,turner2023,rimsky2024} construct them,
though they interpret them through scalar projections or effects on
generation rather than token readouts; \citet{tuomi2026contrastive}
reads such differences through the logit lens, where the shared
component cancels and the distinguishing tokens surface. In each
reading use, a short list of tokens is taken as a description of what
a $d$-dimensional vector contains.

A basic quantitative question about this readout appears not to have
a standard answer: how large must a token-aligned component be,
relative to everything else in the vector, before it surfaces in the
top $k$? The question matters in both directions. If the answer is
``small,'' then a clean token list is compatible with a vector whose
energy is almost entirely elsewhere, and the readout should not be
treated as an energy account. If a component below the answer is
present, the readout will not show it, and its absence should not be
treated as evidence of absence.

This paper derives the threshold under explicit assumptions
(\S\ref{sec:threshold}), measures it with planted signals on five
models (\S\ref{sec:planted}, \S\ref{sec:scale}), characterises where
and why the assumptions fail (\S\ref{sec:scale}), and places one real
contrast on the resulting curve at each scale (\S\ref{sec:anchor}).
All experiments are weights-plus-forward-pass measurements on public
checkpoints; scripts, seeds, and result files are listed in
\S\ref{sec:repro}.

\section{The threshold}
\label{sec:threshold}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be the vector to be read
(a hidden state, a difference of hidden states, or a weight column).
Write it as
\[
x = \sqrt{f}\,\|x\|\,u_t \;+\; \sqrt{1-f}\,\|x\|\,\varepsilon,
\]
where $u_t$ is the unit direction of the $\WU$ row for token $t$, $f$
is the fraction of $x$'s energy aligned with $u_t$, and $\varepsilon$
is a unit vector. The readout computes $|\hat{\WU} x|$ for the
row-normalised unembedding $\hat{\WU} \in \mathbb{R}^{V\times d}$ and
returns the $k$ tokens with the largest values. Token $t$ is
\emph{visible} if it is among them.

\paragraph{Assumptions.} (i) $\varepsilon$ is isotropic; (ii) the
rows of $\hat{\WU}$ behave, against a random direction, like $V$
independent isotropic directions; (iii) the bar is set by the maximum
bath entry ($k{=}1$); a top-$k$ criterion replaces the maximum by the
$k$-th order statistic and relaxes the bar by a constant factor.
Assumption (ii) is the one that fails in practice, in a measurable
way (\S\ref{sec:scale}).

\paragraph{Derivation.} Under (i)--(ii), the bath entries
$\hat{\WU}\varepsilon$ are approximately $\mathcal{N}(0, 1/d)$, and
the maximum of $V$ two-sided Gaussian draws concentrates at
$\sqrt{2\ln(2V)}$ standard deviations. Token $t$'s entry is
$\sqrt{f}\,\|x\|$ against a bath of scale
$\sqrt{(1-f)/d}\,\|x\|$. Visibility requires
\[
\frac{\sqrt{f}}{\sqrt{1-f}} \;>\; \frac{\sqrt{2\ln(2V)}}{\sqrt{d}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f \;>\; \fstar = \frac{2\ln(2V)}{d + 2\ln(2V)}.
\]
For Phi-2 ($d{=}2560$, $V{=}51{,}200$): $\fstar \approx 0.89\%$ and
the bath ceiling is $4.80\sigma$. Since $d \gg \ln V$ in practice,
$\fstar \approx 2\ln(2V)/d$: the \emph{energy} threshold is linear in
$1/d$ (the \emph{amplitude} bar $\sqrt{\fstar}$ scales as
$1/\sqrt{d}$). Because the dependence on $V$ is logarithmic and $V$
is nearly constant across the model suite used below, $\fstar$ is in
practice a function of $d$.

Two consequences follow directly. First, a vector can carry, say,
$90\%$ of its energy in directions unaligned with every token and
still produce a stable, clean top-$k$ list from the remaining $10\%$,
because $10\% \gg \fstar$. Second, averaging $N$ independent vectors
that share the aligned component suppresses the incoherent remainder
by $\sqrt{N}$ in amplitude, so a signal at $f < \fstar$ becomes
visible at roughly $N \approx \fstar/f$.

\section{Planted-signal measurements on Phi-2}
\label{sec:planted}

\paragraph{Method.} A target row direction is planted at energy
fraction $f$ into an isotropic bath,
$\delta = \sqrt{1-f}\,\varepsilon + \sqrt{f}\,u_t$, and we record
whether $t$ lands in the top 15 of $|\hat{\WU}\delta|$. 100 samples
per $f$; content-word tokens; seed fixed. The $f{=}0$ bath maximum
measures the ceiling directly: $4.46\sigma$ against the predicted
$4.80\sigma$, a $\sim$7\% undershoot attributable to correlations
among $\WU$ rows.

\paragraph{Threshold curve.} Hit rates for a single planted token:

\midrule
hit rate & 0.01 & 0.14 & 0.90 & 1.00 & 1.00 & 1.00 \\
median rank & 4119 & 307 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The transition brackets $\fstar \approx 0.9\%$. A 5-token family
signal (the planted energy split over five related rows) shifts the
curve right by roughly the per-token mass split, as expected: hit
rate 0.48 at $f{=}0.01$, 0.91 at $f{=}0.03$.

\paragraph{Triangulation.} Averaging $N$ independent baths sharing a
planted signal at $f = 0.003$ (below threshold):

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
$N$ & 1 & 2 & 4 & 8 & 16 \\
\midrule
hit rate & 0.25 & 0.62 & 0.94 & 1.00 & 1.00 \\
median rank & 135 & 4 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The predicted rescue point is $N \approx \fstar/f = 3$; the curve
crosses between $N{=}2$ and $N{=}4$. This is the arithmetic behind
multi-contrast averaging \citep{tuomi2026contrastive}: it does not
sharpen the signal, it suppresses the bath.

\section{The threshold across scale}
\label{sec:scale}

$V$ is fixed across the Pythia suite (50{,}277 after removing padded
rows) and $d$ spans $5\times$, so the closed form predicts $\fstar$
moves $4.8\times$. The experiment was pre-registered with three
outcome branches (slope $-1$: the closed form holds; slope $-0.5$:
the energy/amplitude exponent is wrong; slope $0$: the max-statistic
model does not govern) and three gates, of which one (the bath
ceiling must be roughly constant in $d$, since it depends only on $V$)
doubles as a per-model isotropy check. A model failing it is
reported and excluded from the regression. 200 samples per point.

\paragraph{Result on the isotropic subset.} For the three models
whose bath sits at the predicted ceiling (spread $0.08\sigma$),
regressing $\log f_{50}$ on $\log d$ gives slope $-0.988$
($R^2 = 1.000$, $n{=}3$). More informative than the slope is the
ratio column of Table~\ref{tab:scale}: $f_{50}/\fstar = 0.56$ at all
three values of $d$, with spread $0.003$. The closed form is correct
up to a single multiplicative constant. The constant being below 1
has the expected sign: a top-15 hit needs to beat the 15th order
statistic rather than the maximum, and the measured ceiling itself
sits $\sim$7\% under the analytic value. We have not derived
it; the scale-invariance of the ratio is the measured content.

Two known effects account for most of the constant. Replacing the
maximum by the $k$-th order statistic moves the bar from
$\sqrt{2\ln(2V)}$ to approximately $\sqrt{2\ln(2V/k)}$, an energy
factor $\ln(2V/k)/\ln(2V) \approx 0.77$ at $k{=}15$,
$V \approx 50\text{k}$; and the bar is set by the \emph{measured}
ceiling rather than the analytic one, a further factor
$(4.46/4.80)^2 \approx 0.86$. Together these predict a ratio of
$\approx 0.66$ against the measured $0.56$; we attribute the residual
$\approx 0.85$ factor to fluctuation of the signal entry itself at
the 50\% crossing (the planted row's logit carries its own share of
the bath noise) and to residual row correlations, and present this as
intuition rather than derivation. The decomposition does predict that
the constant should drift with $k$ and with the measured ceiling,
which is testable.

\paragraph{Five more families.} To move past an $n{=}3$ regression,
we ran the identical sweep on five further models chosen to break the
suite's shared tokenizer and to span vocabulary size:
Table~\ref{tab:scale2}. All five pass the bath-ceiling gate, giving
eight isotropic models across five families, $d = 896$--$2560$ and
$V = 32$k--$251$k. Because $V$ now varies, the right regression is
$\log f_{50}$ on $\log \fstar$ (which absorbs both $d$ and $V$):
slope $1.15$, $R^2 = 0.981$, $n{=}8$. The order-statistic constant
is family-dependent (0.56 for Pythia/Phi, 0.57--0.70 for
the others), which is why the mixed-family slope sits above 1; the
within-family result (slope $-0.99$ in $\log d$, constant $0.56$)
remains the cleanest statement, and the family spread bounds how far
the constant travels: every measured value lies in $[0.56, 0.70]$.

\paragraph{The $V$-dependence, first contact.} The closed form
predicts the ceiling rises as $\sqrt{2\ln(2V)}$: $4.71\sigma$ at
$V{=}32$k, $4.80\sigma$ at $50$k, $5.02\sigma$ at $152$k,
$5.12\sigma$ at $251$k. Across the eight gate-passing models the
measured ceilings correlate with $\ln V$ at $0.88$ and rise
monotonically with $V$ within matched $d$ (BLOOM-560m $4.79\sigma$
vs.\ GPT-2-medium $4.67\sigma$ at $d{=}1024$), sitting uniformly
$4$--$7\%$ under the analytic value, the same undershoot as
Phi-2's and attributable to row correlations. The $V$-term is
directionally confirmed over an $8\times$ span; a quantitative fit
would need more models per $V$.

\paragraph{Where the assumption fails.} Pythia-70m and 160m violate
the ceiling gate at $9.37\sigma$ and $8.34\sigma$, roughly double the
isotropic prediction. The consequence for readability is large: 160m's
measured $f_{50}$ is 14.7\%, five times its analytic $\fstar$, and
70m's hit rate is only 0.38 at $f = 0.3$: a signal carrying 30\% of
the state's energy usually loses the top-15 to the bath.

A follow-up diagnostic (not pre-registered) checked the obvious
explanation, axis-aligned outlier coordinates
\citep{timkey2021,puccetti2022}: it does not hold for this object. On
the row-normalised $\WU$, per-coordinate energy concentration is mild
in all five models (top-1 coordinate share $0.0015$--$0.0065$), and
removing the eight highest-variance coordinates leaves the inflated
ceilings unchanged (70m: $9.37\sigma \to 9.61\sigma$; 160m:
$8.60\sigma \to 8.55\sigma$; the diagnostic re-draws the bath, so its
160m baseline differs from the sweep's $8.34\sigma$ by sampling). What separates the failing models is
the excess kurtosis of the bath distribution: $+1.14$ (70m) and
$+2.15$ (160m) versus $0.00$--$0.01$ for the three passing models.
The small models' unembedding \emph{row directions} are heavy-tailed
in a way not carried by any small set of coordinates; we leave the
precise geometry open. Operationally: the isotropic threshold formula
applies exactly where the bath is Gaussian-tailed, and the bath
kurtosis is a one-line check for whether it applies.

\section{A real contrast on the curve, at each scale}
\label{sec:anchor}

The planted-signal experiments locate the bar; they say nothing about
what real vectors carry, and they construct the isotropic remainder
of assumption~(i) by design. We therefore first measure that
assumption on real vectors, then place a battery of real contrasts on
the curve.

\paragraph{Assumption (i) on real vectors.} For the three isotropic
models at the capture layers below, we take real hidden states,
remove the span of their top-15 $\WU$ rows (the ``signal''), and
measure the remainder's bath statistics through $\hat{\WU}$. The
remainder is \emph{not} isotropic: median excess kurtosis is $2.8$
(Pythia-410m), $19.5$ (Pythia-1b), $3.6$ (Phi-2), with ceilings of
$4.9\sigma$, $6.6\sigma$, $5.5\sigma$ against the $4.8\sigma$
prediction. The same statistic on the real contrast \emph{difference}
below gives kurtosis $4.0$/$6.2$/$8.4$ and ceilings
$4.5\sigma$/$5.2\sigma$/$5.5\sigma$. The practical consequence is a
multiplicative correction: using each model's real-difference ceiling
in place of the random-bath ceiling scales the bar by
$(s_{\text{real}}/s_{\text{rand}})^2 = 1.05$ (410m), $1.32$ (1b),
$1.50$ (Phi-2). The framework survives with a measured correction
factor; the margins reported below clear it comfortably.

\paragraph{The anchor.} As a real anchor we use a minimal contrast
pair inspired by prior work with this readout
\citep{tuomi2026contrastive}: the difference of hidden
states for ``My grandmother said that the \{hot, cold\} dog that she
saw was'' at the final position, captured at the layer nearest 62.5\%
depth in each model. The aligned-energy fraction $f_{\text{real}}$ is
the least-squares span energy of the top-15 $\WU$ rows on the
difference vector; the null is the median span energy of 15 random
rows. The per-model bar is the empirical
$f_{\text{emp}} = s^2/(d + s^2)$ computed from that model's measured
bath ceiling $s$, which is the calibrated bar for the two anisotropic
models. A model's margin is interpreted only if
$f_{\text{real}} \geq 3\times$ its random-span null; below that we
report signal absence rather than a margin.

On the three isotropic models the margin is $9$--$12\times$ the bar
and roughly constant: as $d$ falls from 2560 to 1024 the bar rises
$2.5\times$ and the contrast's aligned energy rises with it
($9.2\% \to 17.3\%$). For this contrast, readability does not degrade
with $d$ in the isotropic regime: bar and signal move together.
The top-15 lists are recognisably about the contrast at all three
scales (e.g., Pythia-1b's top-15 includes \emph{salad, ingredients,
creatures, breeds}).

On the two anisotropic models the contrast is signal-absent: the
top-15 consists of whitespace and byte tokens, and the span energy
sits below even the random-row null (the selected junk tokens' rows
are nearly collinear, so fifteen of them span less than fifteen
random rows). This grouping is expected rather than mysterious: rare
and undertrained tokens retain unembedding rows near their common
initialisation cluster, so a heavy-tailed bath direction that overlaps
the cluster promotes the whole group at once: the readout-side
signature of the undertrained-token phenomenon. With a single contrast we cannot distinguish ``the
model never forms the distinction'' from ``the distinction is formed
and the heavy-tailed bath swamps it''; both are consistent with the
observation. We note only that the two models where the readout
geometry is degraded are also the two where the one real read we have
is empty.

\paragraph{A battery, not an anecdote.} To test whether the anchor is
typical, we ran seven further minimal pairs (soup temperature,
food/tool, animal/vehicle, negation, king/queen, summer/winter,
coffee/water) through the same machinery on all five models. On the
three isotropic models every pair is signal-present ($24/24$), with
median aligned energy $13.0\%$/$8.2\%$/$9.2\%$ and median margins
$6.8\times$/$8.8\times$/$12.0\times$ (range $4.5$--$16\times$);
the hot/cold anchor sits near each model's median, not above it. On
the two anisotropic models every pair is signal-absent ($16/16$),
extending the single-anchor observation: where the readout geometry
is degraded, none of eight ordinary contrasts survives the bath.
These margins are quoted against the random-bath bar; applying the
real-difference correction above ($1.05$--$1.5\times$) leaves all 24
present.

This is still one read position and one seed per pair; the battery
anchors the synthetic curves in eight real measurements per scale
rather than one, but it is not a population claim about what models
of different sizes carry.

\section{What the threshold does and does not license}
\label{sec:implications}

\textbf{Clean readouts are cheap.} Above $\fstar$ (about 1\% of
energy at $d \approx 2500$), a token direction dominates the top-$k$
list. A stable, interpretable readout is therefore compatible with
$\geq 90\%$ of the vector's energy being unaligned with every token
direction, and a top-$k$ list should not be read as an energy
account of the state.

\textbf{Absence is only bounded.} A token missing from the top $k$
carries less than roughly $\fstar$ aligned energy at that layer (up
to the order-statistic constant); it is not evidence of zero signal.
Averaging $N$ matched vectors lowers the detection bar to about
$\fstar/N$.

\textbf{The bar is $\WU$-geometry, not $d$.} The isotropic formula
holds (up to the family constant) on eight of ten models across five
families, including one at $d{=}896$; the two failures are the
smallest Pythias, and what separates them is bath kurtosis, not
dimension. Readout-based claims on small models should be calibrated
against the measured bath of that model's $\WU$, not the dimension
count; claims about real states should apply the measured
real-remainder correction (\S\ref{sec:anchor}).

\section{Related work}

The logit lens \citep{nostalgebraist2020} and tuned lens
\citep{belrose2023} established the readout studied here;
\citet{geva2022} and \citet{dar2022} apply it to weight vectors.
Contrastive and steering methods \citep{zou2023,turner2023,rimsky2024}
construct differences of hidden states but interpret them through
scalar projections or behavioural effects, not token readouts;
\citet{tuomi2026contrastive} reads such differences through the logit
lens, where the shared component cancels and the threshold derived
here governs what surfaces from the remainder.
Anisotropy of learned embedding spaces is well documented
\citep{ethayarajh2019,timkey2021,puccetti2022}, including massive
activations in the residual stream \citep{sun2024}; the measurement
here isolates the specific consequence of unembedding row-direction
anisotropy for top-$k$ readability, and finds it is not the
axis-aligned form in the two affected models. \citet{heap2025} show
sparse-autoencoder interpretability metrics can score well on
random-weight models; the threshold arithmetic is a possible
ingredient of such effects (any readout that only needs $\sim$1\% of
energy aligned will find something to read in many vectors), though
we have not tested that connection.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item The derivation assumes an isotropic remainder and isotropic
  row directions. The second fails measurably on two of ten models;
  the first fails on real hidden states (post-signal kurtosis
  $2.8$--$19.5$), where it is handled as a measured $1.05$--$1.5
  \times$ bar correction rather than derived.
\item The within-family regression has $n{=}3$ points over a
  $2.5\times$ span of $d$; the mixed-family regression has $n{=}8$
  but conflates the family-dependent constant with the slope.
\item The order-statistic constant ($f_{50}/\fstar$; $0.56$ for
  Pythia/Phi, $0.56$--$0.70$ across families) is measured, not
  derived, will vary with $k$, and its family dependence is
  unexplained.
\item The battery uses one read position and one seed per pair; the
  signal-absent results on the two small models are confounded by
  model competence and are reported as absences, not as readability
  failures.
\item Row-normalised readout ($|\hat{\WU}x|$) is used throughout;
  the unnormalised logit-lens variant re-weights ranks by row norms
  and its constant may differ.
\item All models are $\leq 2.7$B parameters. The $V$-dependence of
  the ceiling is confirmed only directionally ($V = 32$k--$251$k,
  correlation $0.88$, uniformly $4$--$7\%$ under the analytic
  value); the $V$-dependence of $f_{50}$ itself is untested
  per-family.
\end{itemize}
